# Policy on generative AI and coding agents

**IDS 575: Machine Learning and Statistical Methods for Business Analytics · Fall 2026**

This policy governs the three take-home assignments. It supplements, and does not replace, the university's academic integrity policy.

---

## The short version

**On assignments, use one of the two approved agents freely, and disclose completely.**
**In the exam room, use nothing.**
**You own everything you submit.**

---

## 1. Why the policy is written this way

You will graduate into work where directing an AI system through an analysis is ordinary. Pretending otherwise in a graduate business course would be a disservice. So the assignments are AI-enabled by design, and they are built to help you become comfortable in working with AI in designing and implementing Machine Learning pipelines.

The trade is that the assignments carry 30% of the grade — 10% each — and the in-person exams carry the other 70%. The assignments are where you learn; the exams are where independent understanding is evidenced. They are also built on the same material as the exam that follows each of them, so treating an assignment as something to get past rather than something to learn from costs twice: once at 10%, and again on the exam it was preparation for.

---

## 1a. The approved agents

**This course uses one of two coding agents: the Codex app or Claude Code. No other coding agent may
be used on coursework.**

Both produce a complete, exportable session history, which is the property the course requires and
is not universal among these tools. Standardizing on two means the setup instructions apply to
everyone, the transcripts are in formats I can read quickly, and help in class applies to the whole
room.

**Codex has a free path** — the Free plan plus the $100 student credit grant OpenAI makes available
to verified students at US and Canadian universities — so nobody needs to spend money to complete an
assignment. Claude Code requires a Claude plan that includes it. Setup for both, and how to claim
the grant, is in `05_setting_up_your_agent.md`.



## 2. Permitted

- Using the Codex app or Claude Code on any part of a take-home assignment.
- Asking for explanations, code, debugging help, alternative approaches, critique of your reasoning, and critique of your writing.
- Using either approved agent, for many sessions across many days.
- Using AI to help you draft the memo, provided the judgments in it are yours and you can defend them.
- Discussing course concepts with classmates.

There is no expectation that you minimise your AI use, and no credit for having used less of it.

## 3. Required

**Work inside the assignment folder.** Each assignment ships as a bounded folder. Keep your work there.

**Export the complete session history for every AI session you use, and submit all of it.** Not a summary, not the interesting parts, not one representative session. If you opened five sessions, all five are submitted. `03_exporting_your_session_history.md` explains how, for each supported tool.

**Validate outputs.** Every number in your memo must be reproducible from your submitted code. If your agent reported a figure you cannot regenerate, it does not go in the memo.

**Document your corrections.** The AI process note asks what you delegated, what you changed or rejected, and how you checked the result. This is the part of the submission that most directly evidences your judgment, and it is graded on specificity.

**Be able to explain and modify your work.** You may be asked, in class or in a short oral check, to walk through a piece of your submission or make a small change to it. This applies to every line you submit regardless of who or what wrote it.

## 4. Not permitted

- **Any generative AI, internet access, or communication device during the in-person midterms and final.** Permitted materials will be announced in advance.
- **Using any coding agent other than the Codex app or Claude Code** on assignments.
- **Concealing AI use, or using a tool whose sessions you do not disclose.**
- **Editing, truncating, or reconstructing a transcript** to change what it shows. Submitting a hand-written "transcript" of a session you did not save is fabrication, not a formatting shortcut.
- **Sharing or receiving code, memos, prompt logs, completed folders, or final answers.** The assignments are individual. Discussing concepts is encouraged; exchanging artifacts is not.
- **Submitting work you cannot explain.**
- **Entering passwords, API keys, or confidential personal or employer data into an AI tool.** All course data is synthetic and safe to share with a model. Your own material may not be.

## 5. What the transcripts are, and are not

**They are an audit trail, not proof of understanding.** A long transcript does not earn marks and a short one does not lose them. The instructor may review logs selectively — typically when a submission's quality and its process note do not match each other, or when a memo contains a claim the submitted code does not produce.

**They are not a second deliverable.** Do not curate them, annotate them, or write them for an audience. Export and attach.

**They are read alongside your process note.** The most common signal of a problem is not a transcript showing heavy AI use; it is a transcript showing that a conclusion arrived fully formed and was never questioned, paired with a process note claiming careful validation.

## 6. If an export goes wrong

Both approved agents produce a complete session history, so the usual cause of a gap is a mechanical
one: a clipboard copy that did not land, a session closed before it was saved, a transcript that
expired. That is recoverable if you say so and unrecoverable if you do not.

If a session you used is missing or incomplete:

1. Submit whatever you do have.
2. Add a short note in `sessions/README.md` stating which sessions are affected, roughly how many,
   what is missing, and what you used them for.
3. Export at the end of every working session from then on, so it does not happen twice.

A disclosed gap is fine. An undisclosed one is a violation.

---

## 7. Privacy warning, read this once

Session transcripts from Codex and Claude Code **embed the full content of files the agent read and the full output of commands it ran**. If you work on assignment material in a directory that also contains something private, that private material can end up inside the transcript you submit.

Do the assignments in a clean folder. Before submitting, open your export and look at it. You are responsible for what is in it.

---

## 8. Consequences

Concealed AI use, fabricated or edited transcripts, and shared artefacts are academic integrity violations and are referred through the university process. Because each assignment is worth 10%, the cost of a violation is enormously out of proportion to anything it could gain. There is no version of these assignments where dishonesty is the rational choice.

If you are unsure whether something is permitted, ask before you do it. Asking has never cost anyone marks in this course.
